# Taiwan Field Archive — Blind Signature Protocol v0.1

> **Purpose:** prevent the Archive from recognizing the story it wants to see.

## 01. Problem

Once an observer has seen labels such as `Taipei`, `TW-001`, or `TRACE-TP-001`, later records are easy to over-interpret.

The blind test removes identity-producing context before comparison.

The question becomes:

> **If we do not know the city, time, hypothesis, or trace label, do some records still look like they belong together?**

---

## 02. What is hidden

During blind comparison, remove:

- city / locality name
- species candidate
- legacy TW number
- trace ID
- observer conclusion
- hypothesis labels such as State Switching / Edge Reading
- literary wording that implies recurrence ("又是牠")

Keep only information needed to understand the behavioral sequence and environmental conditions.

---

## 03. What remains visible

A blind card may contain:

- habitat condition profile
- event sequence
- response latency
- route choices
- waiting / avoidance order
- disturbance and recovery sequence
- whether equivalent alternatives existed
- whether the behavior was environmentally necessary

No morphology.

---

## 04. Comparison task

For each anonymized record, answer independently:

1. Which other record is behaviorally most similar?
2. Which similarity is likely caused by habitat?
3. Which similarity remains after habitat is accounted for?
4. Is there a rare sequence or residual behavior shared by several records?
5. Would you cluster any records together without knowing their origin?

Only after clustering is frozen are labels restored.

---

## 05. Possible outcomes

### PASS — trace survives blinding
Records previously linked to the same trace cluster together for residual reasons not explained by habitat.

Trace confidence may increase one step, never more than one.

### PARTIAL
Some records cluster, others do not. Split the trace candidate or keep confidence unchanged.

### FAIL — labels were doing the work
Once labels disappear, the supposed recurring individual cannot be distinguished from background records.

Trace confidence decreases or trace is dissolved.

### SURPRISE
Records assigned to different traces cluster more strongly than the expected set.

This may reveal observer bias, a population-level behavior, or a mistaken taxonomy.

---

## 06. Anti-cheating rule

Do not construct blind records so that one contains an obviously unique phrase copied from another.

Normalize prose into event sequences first.

Bad:
`牠又多等了那一下。`

Better:
`clearance → no external obstruction → delay → movement`

---

## 07. Trace promotion rule

A trace cannot reach `MEDIUM` solely through repeated literary observations.

To move from `LOW+` to `MEDIUM`, it should survive at least one blind comparison in which:

- habitat-matched distractor records are included;
- labels are genuinely hidden during clustering;
- the signature contains more than one feature;
- the result is recorded even if it fails.

---

## 09. Why failure matters

A failed blind test is not lost content.

It may tell us:

- the behavior belongs to the habitat rather than an individual;
- the behavior belongs to a population rather than an individual;
- two supposed species share a mechanism;
- the observer created continuity through narrative language.

The Archive must preserve these failures.
